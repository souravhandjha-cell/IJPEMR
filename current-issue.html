Development and Optimization of Glibenclamide-Loaded Chitosan Microspheres in a Thermosensitive Nasal In-Situ Gel: In-Vitro Formulation and Characterization
Jaya Pandey¹, Sona Thakur², Akash Kapser³, Souravh Jha⁴, Manisha Chaudhary⁵

¹ ³ Associate Professor, Department of Pharmaceutics and Pharmacology
² ⁴ ⁵ Assistant Professor, Department of Pharmaceutics

Article ID: IJPEMR-2026-001 Original Research Article Volume 1, Issue 1 2026 Open Access Received: 27 July 2026 Accepted: 01 August 2026 Published: 15 August 2026
Abstract

Background: Glibenclamide (GLB) is a poorly water-soluble antidiabetic drug whose oral delivery can be limited by dissolution and first-pass metabolism.

Objective: The objective of this investigation was to formulate, mathematically optimize, and characterize a chitosan microsphere-loaded thermosensitive in-situ gel intended for intranasal delivery.

Methods: Chitosan microspheres were prepared by emulsion cross-linking using glutaraldehyde. A 3² Full Factorial Design was used to optimize chitosan concentration (X1) and glutaraldehyde volume (X2) against particle size (Y1), entrapment efficiency (Y2), and 8-hour drug release (Y3). The optimized microspheres were incorporated into a Pluronic F-127 (18% w/v) and PEG 400 (1.5% w/v) in-situ gel. Particle size, polydispersity, zeta potential, in-vitro drug release, release kinetics, sol-to-gel transition temperature, mucoadhesive strength, and rheological behavior were evaluated. No animal or human studies were included in the revised study scope.

Results: The optimized formulation (F5) showed a mean particle diameter of 42.35 ± 2.15 µm, entrapment efficiency of 84.62 ± 1.85%, and 8-hour drug release of 81.15 ± 1.40%. The formulation showed a zeta potential of +28.4 ± 1.8 mV and PDI of 0.214. The in-situ gel exhibited a sol-to-gel transition temperature of 33.4 ± 0.3 °C and mucoadhesive strength of 4250 ± 112 dynes/cm². Release data showed the highest fit with the Korsmeyer-Peppas model (R² = 0.9945; n = 0.62).

Conclusion: The optimized chitosan microsphere-in-situ gel system demonstrated suitable formulation characteristics, controlled in-vitro release, and thermosensitive gelation properties supporting further formulation development for intranasal delivery.

Keywords: Glibenclamide; Chitosan Microspheres; Design of Experiments (DoE); 3² Factorial Design; Korsmeyer-Peppas Kinetics; Intranasal Delivery; In-Situ Gel.

Extraction, Phytochemical Screening and In Vivo Anti-Diabetic Activity of Herbal Extract
Akash Kapse, Rahul Kumar Gupta

Radharaman College of Pharmacy, Bhopal 462044

Article ID: IJPEMR-2026-002 Original Research Article Volume 1, Issue 1 2026 Open Access Received: 27 July 2026 Accepted: 02 August 2026 Published: 16 August 2026
Abstract

Background: Diabetes mellitus is a metabolic disorder of rising global prevalence, and India carries one of the largest burdens of the disease. Medicinal plants are widely used in folk medicine as low-cost alternatives or adjuncts to synthetic antidiabetic drugs. Luffa echinata Roxb. (Cucurbitaceae) is traditionally used for several ailments, including diabetes.

Keywords: Luffa echinata; antidiabetic activity; alloxan; hydroalcoholic extract; phytochemical screening; flavonoids; phenolics.

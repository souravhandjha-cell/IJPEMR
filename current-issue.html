Biological Evaluation of Antioxidant, Antimicrobial and Preservation Performance of a Herbal Paraben-Free Facial Cleanser
¹Souravh Jha, ²Dr. Yesh Pratap Singh Rana

¹Research Scholar, ²Professor
¹ ²Department of Pharmacy, Monad University

Article ID: IJPEMR-2026-001 Methodological / Conceptual Framework Volume 1, Issue 1 2026 Open Access Google Scholar Ready
Abstract

The increasing interest in botanical cosmetics has encouraged the development of facial cleansing products that combine effective cleansing with skin compatibility and alternative preservation strategies. The present study was designed to evaluate the antioxidant, antimicrobial and preservation performance of a herbal paraben-free facial cleanser formulated using selected botanical extracts. The research framework integrates phytochemical characterization, antioxidant assessment, antimicrobial screening, microbiological quality evaluation and preservation efficacy testing to determine whether a botanical formulation can maintain acceptable quality without conventional parabens. Herbal extracts were selected on the basis of their documented phytochemical composition, traditional cosmetic relevance and reported biological activities. The formulated cleanser was evaluated for physicochemical characteristics before biological testing. Antioxidant performance was proposed to be assessed using complementary radical-scavenging assays, particularly DPPH and ABTS methods. Antimicrobial activity was designed to be evaluated against representative Gram-positive, Gram-negative and fungal test organisms using appropriate controls. Microbial quality assessment was incorporated to determine the microbiological suitability of the finished product, while preservation performance was evaluated using a controlled microbial challenge-testing approach. Particular attention was given to the distinction between intrinsic antimicrobial activity of herbal extracts and the ability of the complete formulation to remain microbiologically protected during storage and repeated use. The proposed evaluation framework recognizes that antioxidant capacity does not necessarily predict antimicrobial effectiveness and that antimicrobial activity of an individual extract cannot substitute for preservative efficacy testing of the finished cosmetic. Stability, pH, surfactant composition, water activity, packaging and interactions among botanical constituents may substantially influence preservation performance. The study therefore provides an integrated approach for assessing the biological functionality and microbiological safety of herbal paraben-free facial cleansers.

Keywords: Herbal facial cleanser; paraben-free cosmetics; antioxidant activity; antimicrobial activity; preservation efficacy; DPPH; ABTS; botanical extracts; cosmetic microbiology; natural preservatives.

Chitosan-Based Microspheres of Glibenclamide Incorporated in In-Situ Gel for Nasal Delivery: Mathematical Optimization, Kinetic Modeling, Ex-Vivo Permeation, and In-Vivo Bioavailability Assessment
¹Souravh Jha, ²Bhupendra Tiwari, ³Mithun Verma, ⁴Suraj Singh, ⁵Gopesh Gunjan, ⁶Dr. V. P. Gupta

¹Assistant Professor, Department of Pharmaceutics
² ³ ⁴ ⁵Associate Professor, Department of Pharmaceutics and Pharmacognosy
⁶Principal, Globus College of Pharmacy, Bhopal

Article ID: IJPEMR-2026-002 Original Research Article Volume 1, Issue 1 2026 Open Access Accepted: 17/08/2026 Published: 01/09/2026
Abstract

Background: Glibenclamide (GLB) is a second-generation anti-diabetic sulfonylurea with poor water solubility (18 µg/mL) and high first-pass hepatic metabolism (>50%), resulting in erratic oral bioavailability (~45%).

Objective: The objective of this investigation was to formulate, mathematically optimize, and evaluate a mucoadhesive, thermosensitive dual delivery system—glibenclamide-loaded chitosan microspheres embedded in a Pluronic-based in-situ gel—for intranasal administration.

Methods: Chitosan microspheres were synthesized via emulsion cross-linking using glutaraldehyde. A 3² Full Factorial Design was used to optimize chitosan concentration (X1) and glutaraldehyde volume (X2) against particle size (Y1), entrapment efficiency (Y2), and 8-hour drug release (Y3). The optimized microspheres (Fopt) were incorporated into an in-situ gel matrix of Pluronic F-127 (18% w/v) and PEG 400 (1.5% w/v). Kinetic drug release modeling, rheological profile, ex-vivo mucosal permeation, histopathology, and in-vivo PK/PD studies on streptozotocin-induced diabetic Wistar rats were conducted.

Results: Response surface methodology yielded Fopt (X1 = 1.5% w/v, X2 = 2.0 mL) with a mean particle diameter of 42.35 ± 2.15 µm, entrapment efficiency of 84.62 ± 1.85%, and 8-hour drug release of 81.15 ± 1.40%. R² values for regression models exceeded 0.98. The gel showed a phase transition temperature (Tsol→gel) of 33.4 ± 0.3 °C and mucoadhesive force of 4250 ± 112 dynes/cm². Release kinetics followed the Korsmeyer-Peppas model (n = 0.62), indicating non-Fickian anomalous diffusion. In-vivo PK revealed Cmax = 142.50 ± 8.20 ng/mL and AUC0-inf = 1485.20 ± 62.40 ng*h/mL for nasal gel versus Cmax = 58.20 ± 4.10 ng/mL and AUC0-inf = 412.30 ± 28.10 ng*h/mL for oral suspension.

Conclusion: The nasal microsphere in-situ gel exhibited a 3.6-fold increase in relative bioavailability (Frel = 360.22%), successfully bypassing hepatic pre-systemic metabolism and providing sustained blood glucose control over 24 hours.

Keywords: Glibenclamide; Chitosan Microspheres; Design of Experiments (DoE); 3² Factorial Design; Korsmeyer-Peppas Kinetics; Intranasal Bioavailability; In-Situ Gel.

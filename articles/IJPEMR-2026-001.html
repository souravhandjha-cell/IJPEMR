Development and Optimization of Glibenclamide-Loaded Chitosan Microspheres in a Thermosensitive Nasal In-Situ Gel: In-Vitro Formulation and Characterization

ABSTRACT

Background: Glibenclamide (GLB) is a poorly water-soluble antidiabetic drug whose oral delivery can be limited by dissolution and first-pass metabolism.

Objective: The objective of this investigation was to formulate, mathematically optimize, and characterize a chitosan microsphere-loaded thermosensitive in-situ gel intended for intranasal delivery.

Methods: Chitosan microspheres were prepared by emulsion cross-linking using glutaraldehyde. A 3² Full Factorial Design was used to optimize chitosan concentration (X1) and glutaraldehyde volume (X2) against particle size (Y1), entrapment efficiency (Y2), and 8-hour drug release (Y3). The optimized microspheres were incorporated into a Pluronic F-127 (18% w/v) and PEG 400 (1.5% w/v) in-situ gel. Particle size, polydispersity, zeta potential, in-vitro drug release, release kinetics, sol-to-gel transition temperature, mucoadhesive strength, and rheological behavior were evaluated. No animal or human studies were included in the revised study scope.

Results: The optimized formulation (F5) showed a mean particle diameter of 42.35 ± 2.15 µm, entrapment efficiency of 84.62 ± 1.85%, and 8-hour drug release of 81.15 ± 1.40%. The formulation showed a zeta potential of +28.4 ± 1.8 mV and PDI of 0.214. The in-situ gel exhibited a sol-to-gel transition temperature of 33.4 ± 0.3 °C and mucoadhesive strength of 4250 ± 112 dynes/cm². Release data showed the highest fit with the Korsmeyer-Peppas model (R² = 0.9945; n = 0.62).

Conclusion: The optimized chitosan microsphere-in-situ gel system demonstrated suitable formulation characteristics, controlled in-vitro release, and thermosensitive gelation properties supporting further formulation development for intranasal delivery.

Keywords: Glibenclamide; Chitosan Microspheres; Design of Experiments (DoE); 3² Factorial Design; Korsmeyer-Peppas Kinetics; Intranasal Delivery; In-Situ Gel.

1. INTRODUCTION

Type-2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM) is a chronic metabolic disorder requiring long-term glycemic control. Glibenclamide (GLB), a lipophilic BCS Class II drug (log P = 3.75), binds to ATP-sensitive potassium (KATP) channel receptors on pancreatic beta-cells to stimulate insulin release.

Despite high potency, oral GLB therapy faces significant bio-pharmaceutical limitations:

  1. Low Aqueous Dissolution Rate: Aqueous solubility is extremely low (0.018 mg/mL at pH 7.4), making dissolution the rate-limiting step for absorption.
  2. First-Pass Hepatic Extraction: Extensive metabolism via hepatic cytochrome P450 isoenzymes (CYP2C9) limits systemic availability to 45-50%.
  3. Fluctuating Plasma Concentration: Transient blood peaks cause sudden hypoglycemic episodes, followed by sub-therapeutic troughs.

The nasal cavity provides a non-invasive administration site with a highly vascularized mucosal surface (~150 cm²), avoiding hepatic pre-systemic metabolism and providing direct entry to systemic circulation. However, mucociliary clearance (MCC) clears simple liquid solutions within 15-20 minutes.

To overcome MCC, this study developed a dual-retention system using chitosan microspheres and a thermo-responsive in-situ gel base.

2. MATERIALS AND METHODS

2.1 Reagents and Materials

Glibenclamide (USP, >99.2% purity), low molecular weight chitosan, Pluronic F-127, PEG 400, glutaraldehyde, light liquid paraffin, Span 80 and glacial acetic acid were used as described in the supplied manuscript.

2.2 Synthesis of Glibenclamide-Loaded Chitosan Microspheres

  1. Aqueous Phase: 1.0-2.0% w/v chitosan was dissolved in 1.5% v/v aqueous acetic acid. Glibenclamide (100 mg) dissolved in 2 mL methanol was mixed under probe sonication (2 minutes, 40 kHz).
  2. Emulsification: The aqueous mixture was added dropwise to 50 mL light liquid paraffin containing 1.0% w/v Span 80, stirred at 1800 RPM for 30 minutes.
  3. Chemical Cross-Linking: Glutaraldehyde solution (1.0-3.0 mL) was introduced dropwise. Cross-linking was maintained at 40 °C for 3.5 hours.
  4. Purification: Microspheres were isolated by centrifugation (4500 RPM, 15 min), washed three times with petroleum ether, washed with deionized water, and vacuum-dried at 40 °C for 24 hours.

3. MATHEMATICAL OPTIMIZATION & STATISTICAL CALCULATIONS (DoE)

A 3² Full Factorial Design evaluated two formulation variables at three levels.

  • X1 (Chitosan Conc. % w/v): Low (-1) = 1.0%, Medium (0) = 1.5%, High (+1) = 2.0%
  • X2 (Glutaraldehyde Vol. mL): Low (-1) = 1.0 mL, Medium (0) = 2.0 mL, High (+1) = 3.0 mL

Second-Order Polynomial Response Equation:
Y = B0 + B1*X1 + B2*X2 + B12*X1*X2 + B11*X1² + B22*X2²

  • Particle Size (Y1) = 42.61 + 8.92*X1 + 3.81*X2 + 1.25*X1*X2 + 1.15*X1² - 0.42*X2²

6. STUDY SCOPE AND LIMITATIONS

The revised study is limited to formulation development, mathematical optimization, physicochemical characterization, in-vitro drug release, release kinetic modeling, and in-situ gel characterization. Animal experiments, human studies, ex-vivo tissue permeation studies, pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic studies, and histopathological evaluations were not included.

7. CONCLUSION

The chitosan microsphere-loaded thermosensitive in-situ gel formulation was successfully optimized using a 3² factorial design and characterized through in-vitro and physicochemical evaluation. The optimized formulation demonstrated suitable particle characteristics, high entrapment efficiency, controlled drug release, favorable release kinetics, and a temperature-responsive gel transition. These findings support further development and future investigation of the formulation for intranasal drug delivery.
